What is GTM?

First, what does go-to-market actually mean?

Go-to-market, or GTM, is how a company finds the people who might need its product and figures out how to reach them.

That usually means answering a few basic questions:

  1. 01

    Who should we talk to?

    Find the right companies, customers, or people.

  2. 02

    What do we know about them?

    Research things like their industry, location, size, job openings, funding, or recent news.

  3. 03

    Who is the right person to contact?

    Find the person responsible and get accurate contact information.

  4. 04

    What should we say?

    Use what you found to write outreach that is actually relevant to them.

Companies have sales and growth teams doing this every day.

Students do a version of the same thing when they recruit for internships, find sponsors for an org, research companies, or reach out to alumni.

The problem is that most of us do it manually.

So where does Clay come in?

Clay turns that work into a system.

Clay is a tool for finding, researching, and organizing information about companies and people.

Instead of opening 50 LinkedIn tabs and copying everything into a spreadsheet, you can give Clay a list and build a process that does much of the work for you.

  1. Step 1

    Start with a list

    Companies, jobs, people, or anything else you want to research.

  2. Step 2

    Add information

    Find things like company size, funding, location, job titles, emails, or recent news.

  3. Step 3

    Filter

    Keep only the companies or people that actually match what you're looking for.

  4. Step 4

    Research

    Use AI and other data sources to answer specific questions about each one.

  5. Step 5

    Reach out

    Create personalized messages using the information you found.

That process is what people mean when they talk about GTM engineering. You are taking work that would normally be done manually and building a repeatable process for it.

No code required.

What would I use this for?

Start with something you already care about.

Get an internship

  • ·Build a list of companies you want to work for.
  • ·Find relevant recruiters, hiring managers, alumni, or people on the team.
  • ·Research each company.
  • ·Find contact information.
  • ·Create personalized outreach.

Example build: Find 100 startups hiring product interns and the right person at each company to contact.

Grow your student org

  • ·Find potential sponsors, speakers, partners, or members.
  • ·Research which ones are actually a fit.
  • ·Find the right contacts.
  • ·Build personalized outreach.

Example build: Find 50 Austin companies that sponsor student organizations and the person responsible for university partnerships.

Research a market

  • ·Build lists of companies in an industry.
  • ·Automatically collect information about each one.
  • ·Track competitors, funding, hiring, products, or other signals.

Example build: Create a live database of AI startups in Austin and track when they raise money or start hiring.
